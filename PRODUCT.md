# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: hiring managers and recruiters for Salesforce, systems, integration and technical-lead roles.** They arrive from a résumé upload (Indeed or similar), LinkedIn, an application's website field, or a cover-letter link. They often decide in about 30 seconds, may be on a work laptop or a phone, and may never play the game. Their job is to answer one question fast: "can this person design and ship systems in our org?"

**Secondary: creative and agency hirers** (creative technologist, WebGL or front-end roles). For them the game itself is the main proof of skill.

The owner is Daniel Donnelly, Technical Lead, Systems, based in Kelowna, BC.

## Product Purpose

A résumé told two ways from one source of content:

1. A first-person 3D game in which the visitor infiltrates a secret facility and recovers intel caches. Each cache opens part of the résumé: projects, skills, bio, résumé and contact.
2. A plain path for people who won't play: a fast, printable résumé page and an ATS-readable PDF résumé that link back to the game.

Success, in priority order:

- the visitor **contacts Daniel** or invites him to interview;
- the visitor **reads or downloads the résumé**, or forwards it internally;
- the visitor **remembers him**: the experience stands out in a stack of identical candidates.

Exploring the whole facility is a bonus, not a goal.

## Positioning

A systems-minded creative technologist: someone who can architect and integrate the unglamorous back end (Salesforce, shipping and customs integrations, ERP data) and also build a delightful, playable front end. The game proves the craft; the case studies prove the systems depth. Neither alone is the claim.

## Operating Context

- Entry points: the uploaded PDF résumé (its header carries the portfolio URL and a QR code), LinkedIn, application website fields and cover letters.
- The title screen offers "Recruiter? Skip the mission → plain résumé", which goes straight to `resume.html` without starting the game.
- In the game: WASD/arrows or an on-screen stick to move, E or tap to interact, Tab for the wristwatch (warp menu, intel tracker, dossier, comms, settings), Esc to pause. Objectives run alien → dossier → hangar projects → vent to SIGINT → data core → rec room.
- The résumé page is read on screen and printed; the PDF goes through applicant-tracking systems and is often printed.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static site: vanilla JavaScript, Three.js and Vite. No framework, no backend. Deployed to GitHub Pages from `main`.
- **`src/content.js` is the single source of content.** The game panels, `resume.html` and `public/resume.pdf` all render from it. Design work must keep it that way.
- `public/resume.pdf` is generated by `npm run resume:pdf` (Playwright) and committed. It must stay **text-based, single column in its reading order, ATS-readable, and two pages or fewer**.
- The game must stay reachable and playable on desktop and touch devices. The plain résumé must work without the game, including without WebGL.
- Settings (audio, CRT effect, sensitivity) persist in `localStorage`.
- Undecided: the employment start date and whether to name the employer (currently a generic description); the LinkedIn URL; real screenshots for project cards; a briefing video (`public/video/briefing.mp4` is absent).

## Brand Commitments

- The game's identity is fixed: a classified spy facility with N64-era first-person feel, CRT overlay, an alien informant, keycards, a vent crawl, an arcade cabinet, and spy/classified vocabulary (operations, dossiers, intel, clearance, comms).
- Refinement, not redesign: work preserves this world. The plain résumé is deliberately quiet and paper-like, and may only borrow lightly from the game.
- Voice: playful in the game, plain and factual on the résumé page and PDF.
- Name: Daniel Donnelly ("Dan Donnelly" on the title screen). Title: Technical Lead, Systems; positioned as "Systems & Salesforce".

## Evidence on Hand

- `src/content.js`: profile, experience, five case studies ("classified operations"), skill groups, four side projects with live links, contact details.
- Live side projects: groc (https://groc-2.vercel.app), Hook Generator, Zodiax and Ski Run on GitHub Pages.
- `public/resume.pdf`; the original prototype in `design/prototype/`.
- **The experience bullets and case studies are drafts written from the owner's notes and session titles, pending his verification.** Design work may restructure and restyle them but must not add new claims, metrics, client names or outcomes; flag anything that needs confirmation instead.
- Absent and must not be fabricated: metrics and quantified outcomes, testimonials, certifications, employer name, client names, education, project screenshots.

## Product Principles

1. **Thirty seconds to the point.** A systems hiring manager can reach the title, summary, case studies and contact within seconds from any entry point, without playing.
2. **The game is the memorable bonus, never the gate.** Every route into the game also has a route out to the plain résumé.
3. **One source of truth.** Content changes happen in `content.js`; no surface forks its own copy.
4. **Proof over polish.** Case studies show problem, what was built and outcome; nothing is claimed that can't be backed up.
5. **The world stays intact.** Improvements make the facility clearer and sturdier, not different.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The résumé page and PDF must meet WCAG 2.1 AA (contrast, headings, keyboard, readable without JavaScript where possible) because they are the primary path for the primary audience. The game should respect reduced-motion preferences, be operable by keyboard alone, and never trap focus in panels.
